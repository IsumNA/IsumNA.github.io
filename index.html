Background

Discipline from the court. Creativity unleashed in code.

For years, competitive badminton meant training every single day — early mornings, repetition, frustration, national-level competition, and medals earned through structure. That environment taught me how to perform under pressure, iterate relentlessly, and hold myself to a standard most people would call obsessive.

The builder mindset

I have always built things — often until 2 or 3am — because the problem will not leave my head until there is a prototype. Friends and family tell me I am crazy sometimes. They are probably right. I am addicted to the loop: sketch, ship, break, fix, repeat.

At Cambridge, I finally have the space to let creativity run wild: merging rigorous engineering with graphic design, UI craft, and a deep fascination with embodied intelligence — systems that perceive, move, and interact with the physical world.

What drives the work

Health technology, human-centred AI, robotics, and interfaces that feel futuristic but practical. I care about software that changes behaviour, not demos that die in a README.

I think in products and systems: how a CV pipeline behaves on a Raspberry Pi in a real oven, how an accountability app creates social stakes, how enterprise AI tools survive messy customer data.

Athletic training gave me structure. Engineering gave me tools. Now I am building the things I used to only sketch at midnight.
